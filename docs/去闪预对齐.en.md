# Deflicker pre-alignment (holy grail)

[中文](去闪预对齐.md) · **English**

In a day-to-night timelapse the camera keeps chasing the light: longer shutter, wider
aperture, higher ISO. What deflicker sees is "scene light × camera compensation" and it
cannot tell which part is real light and which part is just the camera moving -- so it either
flattens the parameter staircase (eating the sunset) or flattens the real darkening (eating
the transition).

**Pre-alignment** strips the camera compensation using the EXIF exposure triplet, runs
deflicker in that scene domain, and folds the gain back into the correction. The result: the
steps are gone, the sunset stays.

## 1. When to turn it on

- Day-to-night or night-to-day (the "holy grail") timelapse, spanning several to a dozen stops;
- you chased the light by hand, or relied on auto exposure to keep the picture from
  blowing out or going black;
- the frames look like "the brightness never really changed" even though you know the sky
  went dark -- that is exactly the transition being eaten by the meter.

On a constant-light scene (indoor stop motion, flat overcast) it does no harm, it just has
nothing to win: the EV staircase is not there.

## 2. How it works: one formula

```
corr = offset + (EV − EV_ref)
```

- `offset`: the per-frame correction from deflicker in the scene domain, trimming **metering
  error** (the ±0.1 stop class);
- `EV − EV_ref`: the **real re-exposure** this shoot performed, `EV_ref` being the median of
  all valid EVs;
- their sum can go far beyond the per-frame ±3 stop clamp. That clamp governs "don't
  over-suppress flicker", not "a re-exposure that was planned" -- exceeding 3 stops on a
  holy-grail sequence is expected, not a bad frame.

Why the anchor is the median rather than the first frame: bad and missing frames never move
it, and -- the practical reason -- the folded total correction then has a **median of zero**,
so pre-alignment never brightens or darkens the whole clip. It redistributes per-frame
exposure without touching total exposure.

## 3. In the panel

Tick "Pre-align on EXIF (holy grail)" in the deflicker panel; a line below reports coverage:

- `128 / 160 frames carry the exposure triplet` -- only frames with an EV are aligned;
- `These frames carry no exposure triplet (ISO / shutter / aperture), so pre-alignment has no
  effect` -- with none at all it **does not pretend to work**, and the other settings
  (window / strength / channels) still apply.

The brightness curve under "Analysis result" is the **folded total correction**, so on a
holy-grail sequence it shows the full day-night range rather than a flattened sawtooth.

The project file remembers the switch: reopening a project, `def_offsets` and the switch share
one convention, never "the numbers are here, the explanation is gone".

## 4. From an agent (MCP)

```
aevum_deflicker_analyze { "align_exif": true }
```

Three fields in the reply matter:

- `align_exif`: which convention was actually used;
- `ev_frames`: how many frames really carried the exposure triplet (0 means a no-op);
- `align_note`: the explanation when it is a no-op, `null` otherwise.

`offsets.min/max/mean_abs` are the folded total correction, same for `series[].ev` -- on a
holy-grail sequence `max - min` is clearly larger than with alignment off. `align_exif` is
remembered too, and `aevum_save_project` writes it into the project.

## 5. Limits and known gaps

- **Frames without the triplet are not aligned and not guessed** (screenshots, PNGs, JPEGs
  whose EXIF was scrubbed by an editor, some phone HDR composites): they become holes for the
  interpolator and their own correction stays 0. Half a staircase in the analysis would read
  as a false jump, which is worse than the gap.
- **No EV at all**: the whole call falls back to plain deflicker -- the switch becomes a
  no-op, but the panel and the reply both say so.
- **Auto-exposure sequences**: the EV staircase mixes the camera's own metering decisions with
  real light, so alignment yields "what fixed exposure would have seen" -- what a timelapse
  wants; but if you deliberately want the breathing of auto exposure, leave it off.
- **Combined with per-channel (RGB)**: all three channels scale by the same gain (camera
  compensation is colour-blind); colour flicker remains the per-channel deflicker's job.
- **`Stats` splits in two**: `jitter_before/after` are reported in the scene domain (notably
  smaller after alignment -- the whole win); `max_ev` / `mean_abs_ev` are the total correction
  including the planned re-exposure, so exceeding `MAX_EV` on a holy grail is normal.

## 6. Assertions

| Assertion | What it bites |
|---|---|
| core `exif_alignment_drops_the_pre_deflicker_residual_...` | the staircase (1 stop) must drop by an order of magnitude to the 0.1 stop metering error; measured as the **largest neighbour jump**, not P95 -- the steps are only 5% of frames and P95 misses them |
| core `the_aligned_output_restores_the_day_night_transition_the_meter_ate` | the output must keep more than 6 stops of transition (under 2 when off). Commenting out the fold turns it red (0.99 stops left) |
| core `missing_evs_become_holes_and_no_evs_at_all_is_a_noop` | hole frames correct to 0; with no EV the result is bit-identical to plain deflicker; pinning the anchor to 0 turns it red (the whole clip shifts by 3 stops) |
| ui `deflicker_align::the_exif_switch_hands_...` | the panel switch really reaches the analysis (unwired it turns red: only 0.97 stops restored); a table one frame short turns it red (the correction table shrinks) |
| mcp `deflicker_align::the_agent_switch_folds_...` | a real JPEG with a real APP1 puts the staircase into the per-frame EV (ignoring the switch turns it red); the `ev_frames` / `align_note` conventions |
| `project_roundtrip` | the switch survives the project file; an old project without the field reads `false` |
